Schools

A school is where most people in India meet a book first. India counts its schools every year, in a census called UDISE+. Almost every public number about Indian schooling starts there. This page is about what that count holds, and what it drops.

The count

The Ministry of Education releases school-level data for every academic year from 2018-19 to 2025-26. From 2022-23 the release covers about 1.47 million schools. We read the header row of all 48 published archives and built a dictionary of every column in them.

The dictionary holds 382 columns. An official Ministry description covers 364 of them. The other 18 are columns the release renamed, and the schema document still describes them under their earlier names. So every column carries the Ministry's own words, and the page says which name those words were written for.

A verified form question covers 181 columns. A person read it off the form, or off a printed grid, or the released values corroborated it. Word-matched leads stay in the machine-readable files with their grade, and off the pages.

Question and variable

A question is what the form asks. A variable is what the release publishes. They are two objects, and the Ministry writes them in two documents that can disagree. The dictionary joins them by matching the release's own description against the form's own words, then checking the answer against the codes the column actually holds.

That check earns its place. Nine columns hold a code the form never defines. In minority_school it covers 418,847 schools in 2022-23 and 428,656 in 2023-24, then disappears. In ict_lab it is the most common value in every year the column exists.

What stopped

The series is not a panel. At the 2022-23 release 79 columns stopped, 33 changed name, and one keeps its name while changing meaning. A study that stacks the years without checking will compare columns that do not hold the same thing.

Of the 79 columns that stopped, 44 are one apparatus. They are the class-by-class count of children admitted under Section 12(1)(c) of the Right to Education Act, the twenty-five per cent quota. That is the only individually justiciable entitlement in Indian school law. From 2025-26 the release carries a single school-level yes or no in its place.

What is asked and never released

Eighteen questions are put to every school in India, every year, and appear in no released column. They cannot be found by browsing the data, because they produced no data. The dictionary lists them.

One of them counts the people who run a school library. Form item 3.1 asks for the number of non-teaching staff by type, and Library Assistant is one of the seven types. It is asked in all nine rounds from 2018-19 to 2026-27 — for 2019-20 we verify this from Delhi's rendition of the round — and is released in no column. The teaching-staff grid printed below it on the same page, item 3.2, is released in full.

How to use it

The dictionary is a reference, not an argument. Look a column up before you use it across years. Each entry carries the years that released it, the wording that produced it, and the comparability notes that a naive read will miss.

The same vocabulary is published as machine-readable linked data, so a paper, a codebook and a replication script can point at one identifier for a column instead of each inventing a private name.

Universities, colleges and other institutions follow. The right to read is delivered through all of them.
